Chapter 38: The Mismatch
========================================

The pre-dawn light of the Sequoias is a bruised purple, filtering through the dense canopy as a nondescript utility van backs into the warehouse with a low, mechanical growl. There are no sirens—just the hushed, efficient movements of the transport team as they offload a sleek, silver-alloy cryo-canister.

By 06:00, the “patient” is already hooked into the array.

The warehouse is silent, save for the rhythmic thrum-hiss of the cooling pumps and the steady drip of condensation from the rafters. Erica and Paul stand at the primary console, their faces illuminated by the eerie blue glow of the neural mapping monitors. Lena stands back, her hands shoved deep into her pockets, her gaze fixed on the flashing data streams.

“Initiating synaptic bridge,” Erica says, her voice steady but thin with exhaustion. “Paul, bring the buffer online.”

“Buffer is live,” Paul replies. “Data stream opening in three... two... one.”

A waterfall of complex, jagged waveforms begins to cascade down the screens. Erica leans in, her brow furrowed. The lines aren’t smooth; they are fractured, sparking with digital noise. She taps a command to recalibrate the mapping laser, her expression shifting to cold, clinical concern.

“The data is non-standard,” Erica says, her voice dropping into a formal, professional register. “Paul, please run a secondary diagnostic on the neural pathway density. The anticipated decay patterns for a male in his late sixties are not present. The cellular crystallization suggests a catastrophic failure in the preservation protocol.”

Paul clicks through a series of 3D renders, his face going pale. “Erica, I’ve run the skeletal architecture scan twice. The cranial volume and bone density markers are consistent with a female subject, likely in her late twenties or early thirties.”

Erica steps back from the console, her posture stiff. She looks at the silver canister with a detached, academic frown. “Lena, there appears to be a significant discrepancy. This subject does not match the medical files provided for the patient. Beyond the poor preservation of the tissue, the biological markers indicate we are currently mapping a young woman.”

Lena feels a jolt of ice in her veins. “Are you certain? Perhaps the preservation process altered the readings?”

“We have verified the data twice,” Erica says, her tone clipped and final. “This is not the patient.”

Lena nods slowly, her mind racing. “Continue the stabilization protocols. I need to inform the client.”

She walks quickly toward the rear of the warehouse, slipping into a small, windowless storage room. She pulls out her phone and types a rapid, encrypted message to Zheng.

Specimen mismatch confirmed by Dr. Voss. Biological markers indicate a young female. Preservation integrity is failing. Awaiting instructions.

A minute passes. Then, the reply pulses on her screen:

The error will be addressed. Do not cease the mapping. I will be at the laboratory tomorrow at 08:00 to resolve this. I am instructing Gonzales to have his full team on-site for the meeting. Maintain the schedule.

Lena exhales a breath she didn’t know she was holding. Zheng is finally coming to the woods. And he’s summoning Gonzales—the Mexican mafia boss who has been providing the muscle and the secrecy—to bring his entire crew into the same room.

She feels the phantom vibration in her right ear. She ducks into the adjacent restroom, locking the door and turning the faucet to a roar. She taps the YouTube icon.

“Smith,” she whispers.

“Go ahead,” the voice vibrates through her jaw.

“The patient is a mismatch. A young woman, badly preserved. But Zheng took the bait. He’s coming here tomorrow morning at eight. He’s ordered Gonzales and his entire organization to be on-site. Everyone will be in the building at once.”

“Copy that,” Smith replies, his voice tight with sudden focus. “This is the ‘Big Fire.’ We’re moving our tactical units into the deep perimeter starting now. We’ll be in position before sunrise. Just keep the peace in that room for another twenty-four hours, Lena. We’re coming for the whole deck.”
