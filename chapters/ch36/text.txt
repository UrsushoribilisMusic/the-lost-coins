Chapter 36: The Morning After
========================================

The morning light in the Sequoias doesn’t so much break as it does seep—a cold, gray mist that clings to the rusted exterior of the warehouse like a damp wool blanket. Inside, the industrial hum of the cooling units has become the heartbeat of the building, but the humans within it are faring less well.

Lena is the first to the small, cramped bathroom in the annex. She barely gets the door locked before the pizza and the stress of her double life make a violent reappearance. She leans over the porcelain, her knuckles white, her breath hitching in the cold air.

A soft knock at the door makes her jump.

“Lena? You okay?”

It’s Erica. Lena splashes cold water on her face, wipes her mouth, and opens the door. Erica is standing there, leaning against the corrugated metal wall, her face a shade of pale that matches her lab coat.

“I’m fine,” Lena lies, though her hands are still trembling. “I think... maybe the pepperoni from that joint wasn’t as ‘local’ as they claimed.”

Erica lets out a dry, pained laugh and slides into the bathroom as Lena exits. “Tell me about it. I feel like my stomach is currently undergoing a synaptic recalibration of its own. If I ever see another cardboard box, it’ll be too soon.”

“Go easy on yourself,” Lena says, leaning against the doorframe. “We have a big day.”

“The biggest,” Erica’s voice muffled from behind the door. “Just... give me five minutes to convince my gallbladder we’re on the same team.”

Despite the rough start, the energy in the main warehouse is electric. The “Big Fire” in the servers has been extinguished, replaced by the steady, rhythmic blinking of green LEDs.

Paul is hunched over his main terminal, his eyes bloodshot but bright with victory. Chen is standing over him, watching the data flow with a mischievous glint in his eye.

“Okay,” Paul mutters, his fingers dancing across the keys. “Primary array is synced. Handshake is at 99.9%. Chen, initiating the final stress test on the neural buffer.”

Chen leans in, squinting at the code. He reaches out and taps a specific line on Paul’s screen, shaking his head solemnly. “Paul. Very bad. You forget the... fēngshàn.”

Paul freezes, his heart skipping a beat. “The cooling fan? I checked the RPMs, they’re nominal. What’s wrong?”

Chen sighs, looking disappointed. “No fēngshàn. System... boom.” He makes a small explosion gesture. “You forgot the ‘Please’ in the command line. Very rude to server. Server angry now.”

Paul stares at him, deadpan, for three full seconds. Then he realizes Chen is holding back a grin.

“You’re pulling my leg,” Paul groans, slumped back in his chair. “You’re actually making ‘polite computer’ jokes while I’m sweating through my shirt?”

Chen lets out a high-pitched cackle, slapping Paul on the back. “Paul too serious! System is perfect. 100% logic. Even without ‘Please’.”

“Go jump in the lake!” Paul laughs, shaking his head. “But the rack is live. We’re ready for the data dump.”

Across the floor, Erica emerges from the annex, looking fragile but determined. She walks toward the massive silver synaptic array—the heart of the machine. She taps a few commands into her tablet, watching the sensors stabilize.

“The calibration run was a success,” Erica announces, her voice echoing through the rafters. “The fluidics are holding pressure, the mapping laser is aligned, and the server rack is hungry.”

She looks at Lena, then at the heavy warehouse doors. The humor of the morning fades, replaced by the heavy, clinical reality of what comes next.

“Paul is right,” Erica says softly. “The system is perfect. All we’re missing now is the patient.”

Lena feels a cold vibration in her right ear. She doesn’t need to check her phone to know the FBI is listening to the silence that follows. The trap is primed, the technology is humming, and the “specimen” is officially on its way.
