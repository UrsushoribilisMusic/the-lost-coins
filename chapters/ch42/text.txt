Chapter 42: The White Witness
========================================

The full moon hung over the Sierras like a cold, silver eye, indifferent to the heat of the guns or the weight of the secrets gathered beneath it. The forest was eerily still, the kind of silence that feels heavy with the pressure of a coming storm.
Inside the mill, the night was a theater of shadows. Two women, separated by three feet of timber and a lifetime of choices, lay awake, staring at the same lunar light.
In the grain cellar, Erica lay on the damp dirt floor, her head resting on Paul’s shoulder. He was asleep, his breathing shallow and hitched from the pain in his ribs, but Erica’s eyes were wide. Through a narrow, iron-grated vent at the very top of the foundation wall, a single shaft of moonlight pierced the dark, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the cold air.
She watched the light, her mind a jagged landscape of “how.”
How did the girl who wanted to map the stars end up in a hole in the ground?
 This wasn’t the life she had designed. She had spent years chasing the perfect neural scan, believing that data was the only truth. Now, she realized she had been so blinded by the promise of a lab and a legacy that she hadn’t seen the bars being built around her.
She looked at Paul’s bruised face in the silver light. She felt a quiet, crushing reckoning. She had pushed him. She had wanted the funding, the prestige, the security. She had traded their peace for a “Whale” that turned out to be a ghost. The turmoil wasn’t a fire; it was a slow, freezing realization that she had become the very thing she used to mock: a mercenary. She closed her eyes, praying to a moon she didn’t believe in that they would see the sun.
Directly above her, in the small, spartan office converted into a sleeping berth, Lena stood by the window. She didn’t turn on the lights. She didn’t need to. The moon was so bright it turned the clearing into a landscape of bone and ink.
She watched the shadows of the Mafia soldiers pacing by the black SUVs, their cigarette cherries glowing like angry stars. She thought she was the only one awake in the entire mill, the only soul burdened by the gravity of the morning.
Her life had been a series of masks—handler, fixer, protector, ghost. She had moved Paul and Erica like pieces on a board, telling herself it was for the mission, for the “greater good.” But tonight, the mask felt heavy. She thought of the way Erica had looked at her before the guards dragged them down—the look of a woman who had trusted a sister and found a viper.
A pang of genuine, visceral guilt twisted in Lena’s chest. She had misled them, yes. She had lied. But as she watched that same moon, she felt her life changing shape. The cold professionalism was cracking. For the first time, she wasn’t thinking about the payload or the extraction. She was thinking about the two people in the dark beneath her feet.
I’m sorry,
 she whispered to the empty room, her breath fogging the glass.
She didn’t know that three feet below, Erica was staring at the same silver glow, both of them caught in the same orbit, both realizing that by dawn, the women they used to be would be gone forever.
