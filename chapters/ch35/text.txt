Chapter 35: The Last Supper
========================================

The hum of the synaptic array is a constant, low-frequency vibration that seems to settle in the very marrow of their bones. As the sun dips below the canopy of the Sequoias, plunging the logging site into a deep, prehistoric darkness, the warm, yellow light of the annex spills out onto the damp needles of the forest floor.
Inside, the “intermission” of Napa has been replaced by an industrious, almost collegiate energy. The living quarters are Spartan—cots, a scarred wooden table, and a small kitchenette that smells of damp cedar. But tonight, the atmosphere is strangely relaxed.
Lena had returned from town with the medical supplies and several stacks of cardboard boxes from a local pizza joint. The incongruous smell of pepperoni and melted mozzarella fills the industrial space, a mundane scent in a room housing millions of dollars in cutting-edge technology.
Paul and Chen are sitting on equipment crates, sharing a large pie. They’ve moved past the “big fire” glitches and are now comparing photos of their pets on their phones. Paul laughs as Chen shows him a particularly grumpy-looking ginger cat in Shanghai.
“See?” Paul says, gesturing with a crust. “Logic is universal, but cat memes are the true global currency.”
Across the table, Erica is sitting with the other two engineers. The professional frost has thawed. She’s using a marker to draw a simplified neurological map on a paper napkin, explaining the final calibration steps. The engineers nod, their eyes bright with the shared language of high-level biology. For a moment, they aren’t international contractors; they are a team on the verge of a miracle.
“To a good day of work,” Paul says, raising a warm bottle of beer Lena had managed to snag in town.
“To the work,” Erica echoes, her eyes meeting Lena’s. There is a softness there, a lingering memory of the villa that the cold fluorescent lights can’t quite kill.
The moment is interrupted by a steady, rhythmic thud of boots on the warehouse floor. A man steps into the light of the annex. He’s built like a slab of granite, wearing a dark canvas jacket and heavy work boots. While his presence is imposing, his expression is one of professional patience—the look of a man who manages high-stakes logistics for a living.
He stops at the edge of the table, his eyes moving over the group before landing on Lena. “The transport teams are asking for an update,” he says, his voice a controlled, baritone rumble. 
The table goes quiet. Paul pauses with a slice of pizza halfway to his mouth, the reality of the “patient” suddenly bringing the weight of the project back into the room.
Lena stands up, her “concierge” poise snapping back into place like a shield. She moves toward the man with a graceful, welcoming gesture. “Erica, Paul—this is Marco. He’s the security manager for the site. He’s the one responsible for the safe transit of the patient.”
Marco offers a short, respectful nod to Erica. He doesn’t look like a thug; he looks like a man who takes his job very, very seriously. “Dr. Voss. It’s an honor to see the facility finally humming.”
Erica wipes her hands on a napkin and stands up to face him. She doesn’t flinch at his size; the scientist in her is too focused on the data to be intimidated.
“We’ve cleared the latency issues in the primary bus,” Erica says, her voice echoing in the rafters. “The synaptic mapping is ninety percent calibrated. We’ll be running the final stress tests tomorrow.”
She looks at the gleaming silver array, then back at Marco with a look of exhausted triumph. “Tell your teams they can move. The system is stable. We will be ready for the patient by tomorrow evening.”
Marco nods once. “Tomorrow evening. I’ll relay the word. Enjoy your dinner.”
He turns and vanishes back into the shadows of the main warehouse. Lena feels the phantom thrum in her right ear. She hadn’t triggered the app, but she knows the phone on the table has caught every word. She looks at the half-eaten pizza and the tired, hopeful faces of her team. The “last supper” is over. Tomorrow, the ghost they’ve been chasing finally arrives.
