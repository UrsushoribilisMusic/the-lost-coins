Chapter 25: The Shadow on the Vine
========================================

Chapter 25: The Golden Hour Intermission
The black Escalade winds through the rolling, golden hills of Napa, a stark contrast to the dense, technological fog of Zug. Inside the cabin, the air conditioning hums a low, expensive tune, but the scent of sun-baked earth and ripening grapes still manages to seep through the vents. The mood is lighter, the sterile pressure of the Swiss lab replaced by the lush, sprawling freedom of the valley.
Paul is glued to the window, watching the endless rows of grapevines blur past. He looks younger in the California light, the permanent crease of worry between his brows finally smoothing out.
“The client really knows how to handle a delay,” Paul says, leaning back into the hand-stitched leather. He catches Erica’s eye and grins. “I could get used to ‘Customs issues’ if it means a holiday in a wine yard before we disappear into work.”
Erica nods, but her gaze doesn’t linger on the scenery. It remains fixed on Lena, who sits in the front, her profile silhouetted against the bright vineyard sun. Erica’s curiosity has shifted from the logistics of the project to the woman managing them.
“He’s certainly generous,” Erica says, her voice trailing over the words. “But it’s a curious contradiction. A man who can build a lab in California and secure medical permits usually doesn’t get tripped up by port bureaucracy. Don’t you think, Lena?”
Lena doesn’t miss a beat. She watches the road, her expression unreadable behind her sunglasses, but her tone is intimate—designed to soothe. “Wealthy donors are eccentric, Erica. They over-prepare for the science because they understand it, and they under-prepare for the bureaucracy because they despise it. That’s why I’m here. To bridge the gap between his vision and the world’s red tape.”
Lena’s phone buzzes in her lap. A brief, encrypted ping from the voice in Macau.
Equipment delayed at Port of Oakland. 48-hour window. Stall them. Keep the talent happy.
Lena hides the screen. “Actually,” she continues, her voice dropping into a softer, more conspiratorial register, “I think the client realized we’ve all been moving too fast. He wanted us to have this. A moment to breathe before the real work begins. He knows you’ve both sacrificed a lot to get here.”
Erica watches the way Lena’s hair catches the light. The suspicion is still there, but it’s being crowded out by something else—a growing fascination with Lena’s poise, and the way she seems to anticipate their every need.
Meanwhile, in a nondescript office park in San Francisco, the air is cold and smells of burnt coffee and ozone. Inspector Pinto stands over a digital map of Northern California, his eyes fixed on a pulsing red dot.
A tall man in a dark suit enters the room, flashing an FBI badge. “Inspector Pinto? I’m Special Agent Smith. I will coordinate the activities here.”
“They’re heading North,” a technician says, clicking a mouse. “Napa.”
Pinto’s eyes narrow. He looks at the map, then at the surveillance photos of Lena from the airport. “Captain Meier was right—Lena is the anchor. If she’s relaxed, the doctor and the hacker stay relaxed. She’s the one holding the leash.”
 Smith says: “If they’re heading to Napa, we have a window. We let them get comfortable in the vineyards. We wait until they think they’re safe.”
Pinto looks at the map. The red dot is slowing down, turning into a private estate. “We should be strategic, Agent. The moment they realize they’re being watched, Mr. Z. will cut them loose. And if he cuts them loose, we lose the chance to get the big guys.”
Back in the Escalade, the gravel crunches as the car pulls into the long, gated driveway of a limestone villa. The scent of lavender is overwhelming as the doors open. For a split second, Lena feels a sudden, sharp chill—the phantom sensation of being watched. She looks in the rearview mirror, but the road behind them is a shimmering, empty ribbon of heat.
She turns to Erica and Paul, her smile now warm and inviting, a perfect imitation of a host welcoming friends.
“We’re here,” Lena says. She catches Erica’s gaze and holds it just a second too long for it to be purely professional. “Welcome to Napa.”
