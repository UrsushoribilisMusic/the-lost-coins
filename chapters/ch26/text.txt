Chapter 26: The Decoy
========================================

The Scottsdale midnight is silent. Jose and Juan move through the Alcor Primary Readiness Wing. They aren’t here for a ghost yet; they are here for the vessel. To pull off a seamless swap later, they need a brand-new, sterile Dewar that looks exactly like a fresh “new arrival” to sit in the pioneer’s place.
“Quiet,” Jose whispers, his breath hitching as they wheel a gleaming, heavy stainless steel canister—Unit 803—toward a service elevator.
They don’t leave the building. Instead, they take the unit down to a sub-level maintenance room, a “blind spot” in the facility’s camera grid where the nitrogen lines are being serviced. Waiting there in a nondescript medical bag is the body of a young woman, a tragic casualty of the Phoenix streets whose “donation” was brokered by Gonzales.
With the clinical detachment of butchers, they load her into the Apex-series tank. They seal the vacuum jacket, prime the nitrogen levels, and prep the unit until it looks like a legitimate, high-priority patient.
“Weight is perfect,” Juan grunts, checking the digital readout. “No one’s going to notice a ten-gram difference once it’s in the rack.”
They wheel the now-occupied Unit 803 back into the storage wing, slotting it into a standby position near the high-security vault. It sits there, a ticking heart of ice, waiting for the day of the final heist.
It isn’t until an hour later that Miller, the Alcor Security Boss, sees the “blip” on his log.
He sits in his office, reviewing the automated weight-sensor reports. For a few minutes Unit 803 had registered as “missing” from its dock, followed by a slight pressure fluctuation in the sub-level maintenance bay.
“Hey, check the log on 803,” Miller calls out to the junior tech. “It moved and then reappeared an hour later.”
The tech taps at his keyboard, his eyes tired. “Probably just the maintenance crew doing the pre-chilling cycle, Boss. They’re scheduled to move the new Apex units to the staging area for that ‘Gold-tier’ arrival on Thursday.”
Miller looks at the screen. The cameras show the hallway is empty now. He sees the “return” of the tank on the digital map.
“Probably,” Miller mutters, rubbing his eyes. “Just a sensor blip. These new Apex units are so sensitive they trigger an alert if someone breathes too hard on the docking clamps.”
He dismisses the alert with a single click. In his mind, it’s just a mundane equipment movement—the kind of “glitch” he’ll only remember three days from now, when the pioneer’s real tank disappears and he realizes the “new arrival” in the vault is a fake.
