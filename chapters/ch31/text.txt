Chapter 31: The last mile
========================================

The drive from Napa to the Bay Area is bathed in the clean, mid-morning light of the California coast. Inside the black Escalade, the air is thick with a different kind of energy than the flight from Zurich. The professional distance has been replaced by a comfortable, post-intimacy hum.
Lena sits in the front, but her focus is on the two in the back. Through the rearview mirror, she sees Erica’s hand resting naturally on Paul’s knee. They look... different. The sharp edges of their anxiety have been blunted by the luxury of the villa and the weightless hours of the previous night.
“I have to say,” Lena says, her voice warm and genuinely soft, “watching the two of you this morning... you fit together better than I even imagined. There’s a balance there. It’s rare to find that in a partnership, especially under this kind of pressure.”
Paul looks over at Erica and beams, the look of a man who has finally won the lottery. “It was... exactly what I needed. What we both needed. Thank you, Lena. For the villa, for the wine... for everything.”
Erica leans forward, her eyes meeting Lena’s in the mirror. There is no trace of the predatory dominance from the terrace—only a lingering, satisfied glow. “I’ll admit, I was skeptical,” Erica says. “I expected the evening to be... complicated. Messy. But I was surprised at how perfectly it all went. It felt right.”
She pauses, her gaze holding Lena’s for a beat longer than necessary. “I’d love an encore. Once the equipment is settled... I want to find that terrace again. With both of you.”
Lena feels a cold spike of dread beneath her smile. 
Once the equipment is settled.
 She knows Zheng is in the air. She knows the “encores” in this business are usually served in windowless rooms. “I’d like that, Erica. More than you know.”
As the skyline of San Francisco begins to jag upward against the horizon, the car slows near the Claltrain Station.
“This is us,” Paul says, grabbing his laptop bag. He leans over and gives Lena a quick, affectionate squeeze on the shoulder. “Don’t work too hard in the city. We’ll see you tomorrow night after the Stanford talk?”
“Count on it,” Lena says.
Erica lingers for a second. She leans in, pressing a quick, lingering kiss to Lena’s cheek. She watches them step out of the car—the brilliant scientist and the loyal hacker, heading toward a world of academic prestige and “future plans.” They look like a couple with everything to lose.
As the Escalade pulls away toward Nob Hill, Lena lets out a long, shaky breath. She pulls out her phone to check the room ready status at the Fairmont. She doesn’t see the nondescript silver sedan following her.
She is thinking about the ultrasound of her sister’s baby. She is thinking about Erica’s “encore.” She is thinking about everything except the fact that in forty minutes, she will be walking into Room 412, where Inspector Pinto and Special Agent Smith are already sitting in the dark, waiting to dismantle her life.
