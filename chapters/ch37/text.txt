Chapter 37: The Shell Game
========================================

The fog in San Francisco is so thick it looks like the city is being erased, block by block. High above the gray shroud, in a penthouse suite at the St. Regis, Zheng stands by a floor-to-ceiling window. He isn’t looking at the view; he is looking at a series of encrypted data streams flickering across a tablet in his hand.
He taps a command, opening a video bridge to Gonzales.
Gonzales appears on the screen, sitting in the back of a darkened SUV somewhere in the East Bay. The interior is lit only by the green glow of his tactical tablet.
“The scientists are done,” Gonzales reports. “The array is calibrated. Lena says the doctors are ready for intake.”
“Good,” Zheng says, his voice a cold rasp. “We move the Whale tonight. But we do not move in a straight line. I want the decoy team to pull the heat.”
“Already in motion,” Gonzales says. “The armored transport is staged at the Oakland facility. It’ll leave at 02:00, heading west toward the coast with a full security detail. If the authorities are watching the ports or the roads, they’ll follow the noise. The real canister—the patient—will stay in the nondescript utility van. It’ll take the back roads through the valley and arrive at the warehouse just before dawn.”
“Ensure the transfer is seamless,” Zheng commands. “The scientists are not to see the security maneuvers. To them, this is a delicate medical delivery. If they feel the weight of a paramilitary operation, they will lose their focus. And I need Dr. Voss focused.”
“Understood,” Gonzales says. “I’ll stay back to coordinate the handoff. I’ll see you on the other side of the transfer.”
The screen goes black. Zheng sets the tablet down and stares at his own reflection in the glass. He has spent years and a small fortune for this moment. The “patient” is the key to a door that hasn’t been opened in human history, and he is finally close enough to touch it.
Back at the warehouse, the air is thick with the smell of damp pine and the lingering scent of ozone. The day’s frantic technical energy has settled into a quiet, exhausted lull.
Lena arrives back from a quick run to a nearby trailhead, carrying bags of fresh salads for Erica and Paul, and several containers of steaming Sichuan food for Chen and the engineers. It’s a peace offering for a team that has been pushed to the edge of their capabilities.
As they sit around the scarred wooden table in the annex, the mood is almost serene. Paul and Chen are debating the merits of different spicy oils, while Erica picks at her greens, her eyes periodically drifting to the synaptic array in the main room.
Lena’s phone buzzes in her pocket. She pulls it out, the blue light reflecting in her eyes. It’s a text from the man in San Francisco.
The patient is in transit. Expected arrival: 05:00. Ensure the sterile field is maintained and the team is rested. This is the final phase.
Lena sets the phone down, her heart doing a slow, heavy roll in her chest. She looks at Erica and Paul—so brilliant, so hopeful, and so utterly unaware that they are about to become accomplices in a crime that has no name.
“Everyone,” Lena says, her voice steady and professional, cutting through the low chatter. “I just got word from the client. The patient is arriving tomorrow at dawn.”
Erica stops eating, a look of sharp, clinical focus returning to her face. “Tomorrow morning? We’ll need to run a final purge of the cooling lines before then.”
“Then let’s finish dinner and get some sleep,” Lena says, forcing a warm smile. “The ‘intermission’ is officially over. Tomorrow, the real work begins.”
She catches her reflection in the dark glass of the annex window. She knows Smith is out there, somewhere in the fog, waiting for the GPS ping that will tell him the Whale has landed. She just hopes she’s still on the right side of the glass when the doors finally blow open.
