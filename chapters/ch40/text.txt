Chapter 40: The Swap
========================================

The high-definition video link on the monitor flickered, casting a pale glow over the mahogany desk in Gonzales’s safe house. Zheng’s face was a mask of controlled fury, his voice a thin blade of ice transmitted from a hotel suite in San Francisco.
“I am not interested in biological anomalies, Gonzales,” Zheng hissed. “I am interested in results. Dr. Voss says the specimen is a mismatch. This is a catastrophic failure of logistics. I want an onsite meeting. We will resolve this at the lab, in person, tomorrow at 10:00 sharp.”
Gonzales tapped a key, ending the call with a sharp snap. He stood up and walked into the adjacent living area, where the two transporters—the men responsible for the utility van’s cargo—were waiting. They looked small against the heavy curtains of the safe house, their eyes darting nervously toward their boss.
“Explain it to me,” Gonzales growled, stepping into their space. “How does a sixty-year-old billionaire turn into a thirty-year-old woman in the span of a three-hour drive? Did the mountain air do that, or am I being lied to?”
The lead transporter, a man with a jagged scar across his knuckles, wiped sweat from his upper lip despite the air conditioning. “Boss, we did exactly what you said. We had the two canisters at the staging point. We had to move fast—the sensors were already pinging.”
“And?” Gonzales barked.
“We moved the Finnley ID onto the canister we were taking,” the second man insisted, his voice thin. “We checked the labels. We grabbed the one marked ‘Target.’ We did as instructed.”
Gonzales stared at them, his eyes narrowing. He thought of the billions sitting in that Bitcoin wallet—money that could buy him an empire far beyond the Sierras. He wasn’t going to let a couple of low-level screw-ups cost him the biggest score in history.
“The computer says otherwise,” Gonzales spat. “It says you brought me a random sleeper from the Alcor overflow because you couldn’t keep track of your own shell game. You idiots got confused by your own decoys, and now Zheng thinks I’m trying to play him.”
He checked the gold watch on his wrist. “Get your gear ready. We move out tomorrow morning. I expect both of you to be in the car with me. We’re going to be at that mill by 09:45. If that canister is a mismatch, we’re going to find out where the real one went before Zheng gets any ideas about cutting me out of the profit.”
Gonzales leaned in close, his voice dropping to a lethal whisper. “I’ve invested too much blood in this ‘Whale’ to lose it to a clerical error. Make sure your weapons are clean. Tomorrow, we get what’s ours—one way or another.”
He turned and marched back into his office, slamming the door.
The two transporters stood in the sudden silence. The lead man looked at his partner, his expression shifting from fear to a deep, nagging doubt. He leaned in, his voice barely a breath.
“Hey,” he whispered. “You were on the rear door the whole time we were at the staging site, right? You never let that van out of your sight?”
The second man hesitated. He looked at the closed office door, then back at his partner. His eyes shifted toward the floor, a flicker of uncertainty crossing his face as he remembered the brief moment he’d stepped behind a pine tree, his back turned to the open loading bay.
“Yeah,” he muttered, though the word lacked conviction. “I was right there. I... I’m pretty sure I was right there. The tags were right, man. They had to be right.”
“Then how did we end up with the wrong body?” the first man hissed.
They stood there for a long moment, the silence of the safe house feeling heavier than before. Neither of them had an answer, and both knew that with Gonzales’s greed now fully ignited, there would be no room for mistakes tomorrow.
