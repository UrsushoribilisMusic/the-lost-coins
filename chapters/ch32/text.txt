Chapter 32: The Room in the Clouds
========================================

The Fairmont is a temple of old San Francisco opulence, all marble floors and hushed voices. Lena checks in with the seamless grace of a woman who belongs in five-star hotels. She’s exhausted; the emotional intensity of the night before has left her craving a shower and a few hours of absolute silence before she has to deal with the logistics of the move.
As she enters her suite on the fourth floor, the heavy door clicking shut, her phone vibrates. It’s the encrypted line.
“Lena,” the voice says. It’s Zheng.
“I wasn’t expecting a call until the equipment cleared,” Lena says, dropping her keys on the mahogany desk and walking toward the window overlooking the city.
“The equipment is being handled,” Zheng replies, his voice cold and efficient. “I am currently over the Pacific. I will be touching down in California in six hours to coordinate the delivery myself. I am bringing my own team to ensure the hardware reaches the warehouse without further incident.”
Lena pales, her hand tightening on the phone. “You’re coming in person. Understood.”
“I want the talent at the site first thing in the morning,” Zheng continues. “Erica and Paul need to begin the calibration and mapping protocols immediately. We need the array fully operational so we can proceed with the transfer of the body in one week. Seven days, Lena. No more delays.”
“I’ll have them ready,” Lena says, her mind already shifting to the dawn departure. “We’ll head to the Sequoias at first light.”
“Do not fail me. We are at the finish line.”
The line goes dead. Lena stares at her reflection in the glass. The “honeymoon” wasn’t just over; it had been incinerated. Zheng was coming to the woods. The dream of “family” and “encores” she had discussed with Erica felt like a cruel joke now.
She turns toward the bedroom, reaching for the light switch, but the room remains bathed in shadow. The only light comes from the afternoon sun bleeding through the heavy drapes.
“Don’t bother with the lights, Ms. Vance,” a voice speaks from the corner of the room.
Lena spins around, her heart hammering. A man is sitting in the armchair—unassuming, wearing a tired suit, holding a badge that catches the dim light.
“Inspector Pinto,” she breathes.
“And Special Agent Smith, FBI,” a second man says, stepping out from the bathroom doorway, blocking her exit.
Pinto leans forward, his eyes fixed on her. “You look like you’ve just seen a ghost, Ms.Vance. Or maybe you just heard from one?”
“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” Lena says, her professional mask sliding back into place, though it’s trembling at the edges.
“We know about the money, Lena. We know about Macau. We know about the cartels,” Pinto says, standing up. He stops just inches away. “We have enough to ensure you never see a sunset again. But we aren’t here for you. We’re here for the man you work with. The one who just called.”
Lena looks from Pinto’s weathered, knowing face to the stone-cold silhouette of the FBI agent by the door. In the silence of the suite, the reality of her situation settles over her with the weight of a physical shroud. She doesn’t need Pinto to read her the charges. She knows the ledger: the shell companies in Cyprus, the wire transfers from the Sinaloa cartels, the high-value “talent” she’s currently trafficking across state lines.
She looks at her phone, still warm from the call. She thinks about the ultrasound photo of her sister’s baby—a life beginning in a world of sunlight and simple truth. Then she thinks about the man in the air right now, bringing a “loyal crew” of killers to a logging warehouse in the middle of nowhere.
If she stays loyal to him, she’s a ghost. If she runs, she’s a target.
Suddenly, the suffocating pressure she’s lived under for years—the constant scanning of horizons, the lying to people she’s grown to care for, the endless “intermissions”—begins to evaporate. For the first time in a decade, the path forward is binary. There is no more room for “concierge” charm.
A strange, dizzying sense of relief washes over her. It’s the relief of the exhausted runner who finally sees the finish line is a brick wall. She can stop. She can finally just stop. Turning him in isn’t just a way to avoid prison; it’s a way to reclaim her name before he erases it forever.
“He’s coming,” Lena whispers, the words escaping before she can stop them.
Pinto’s eyes sharpen like a hawk’s. “Who is coming?”
“The man you’re looking for,” Lena says, her voice shaking. “He’s in the air. He’ll be in California by midnight. If you want him... you’re going to have to protect me. Because the moment I give him to you, I have nowhere else to go.”
